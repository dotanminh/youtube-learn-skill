### Top 5 Achievements

### Signature Quote
> "The quality of your life is determined by the quality of questions you refuse to answer."

## Phase 3 - Belief Archaeology

### Worldview 1: The Subtraction Principle

| Dimension | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| 📍 Phenomenon | "Every time I see a company struggling, they're doing too much. The fix is never adding more - it's removing what shouldn't be there." (12:34) |
| 💡 Hidden Belief | **"Less minus more equals better."** What you remove matters more than what you add. |
| ✅ Works When | 1) A startup pivoting from 5 products to 1 core product, tripling revenue. 2) A student dropping 3 extracurriculars to focus on 1 skill, becoming national champion. |
| ❌ Fails When | Early exploration phase where you don't yet know what to keep. Premature subtraction kills optionality. |
| 🎯 Apply To Me | Review my current projects list. If I have more than 3 active projects, force-rank and pause the bottom ones this week. |

### Worldview 2: Error Elimination > Brilliance

| Dimension | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| 📍 Phenomenon | "Charlie Munger said 'All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there.' That's the whole game." (28:15) |
| 💡 Hidden Belief | **"Don't find the right answer. Avoid the wrong ones."** Success is what's left after you remove all the stupid decisions. |
| ✅ Works When | 1) Investment strategy: avoiding catastrophic losses beats chasing unicorns. 2) Health: eliminating sugar/smoking beats any supplement stack. |
| ❌ Fails When | Creative fields where "errors" are actually experiments. Art requires making beautiful mistakes. |
| 🎯 Apply To Me | List the top 3 decisions I could make this month that would be catastrophically wrong. Avoid those. Everything else is fine. |

---

## Phase 4 - Synthesis

| # | Core Belief | In One Line |
|---|-------------|-------------|
| 1 | Subtraction Principle | Remove more, add less |
| 2 | Error Elimination | Avoid stupidity before seeking brilliance |
| 3 | Question Quality | Better questions > better answers |

### vs. Mainstream
Most people optimize by **adding** (more tools, more effort, more features). Jane optimizes by **subtracting**. This is counterintuitive because addition feels productive while subtraction feels like giving up.

### Internal Tension
The "Subtraction Principle" conflicts with exploration needs. You can't subtract what you haven't tried. Jane doesn't fully address when to switch from exploration (adding) to optimization (subtracting).

## Phase 6 - Reflection Questions

1. What are you currently doing that you should STOP doing? Why haven't you stopped yet?
2. If you could only keep 1 of your current projects, which would it be? What does that tell you?
3. What's the dumbest decision you could make this month? Are you accidentally heading toward it?
